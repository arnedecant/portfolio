I'm Arne, a Belgium-based senior software engineer with more than a decade of experience, mostly focused on the front end. I like working on the parts of software where a lot is happening at once: complex interactive tools that still need to feel reliable, clear, and calm to use.

My work sits between engineering, interaction design, and product thinking. I care about how something looks, how it behaves, and whether the structure underneath will still make sense when the product has to evolve.

> I like complex systems best when the complexity is doing useful work, not asking the user to carry it.

## A gradual move toward the front end

I always knew I wanted to work on the web, but I did not start out as a front-end specialist. That became my focus gradually, through jobs that let me see more of the whole problem each time.

### 2014–2018 / RIS

My first job was at a small agency, building WordPress sites for local police, event organisers, businesses, portfolios, and many others. I handled most of the lifecycle:
- designing and developing the sites
- taking them live and keeping them running
- working on SEO and email campaigns

Owning those steps taught me how the pieces fit together. It also made switching between design thinking and implementation feel natural. This is where CSS and SCSS really hooked me: they went from “just styling” to something I wanted to understand properly. My JavaScript was mostly simple jQuery at the time, but the seeds were planted.

### 2018–2020 / Nanopixel

At Nanopixel, I moved into more product-like interactive work. The company brought together space, visualisation, and technology, and I worked mainly on Showreal, a sales tool for real-estate showrooms. It was a kiosk setup with a touch screen and a wall of TVs, powered by web technology and Node.js behind the scenes.

Some things I am especially proud of there:
- helping build Showreal from the ground up
- creating a small custom framework for the tool
- solving unusual kiosk and touch problems, including a custom physical cylinder with specific touch-points used for navigation

I also worked closely with an experienced freelancer who pushed my JavaScript forward. We did not have linters or fancy tooling, but we agreed on how to write clean code and followed those agreements consistently. Over time, our code became indistinguishable. That collaboration shaped how I think about code quality, consistency, and communication.

### 2020–now / Kiswe

I have always been drawn to multimedia. When I started studying, I was genuinely torn between video production and development. Working at Kiswe on tools for live-stream production feels like those two paths finally meeting.

I work mainly on Kiswe Studio, and sometimes Kiswe Command Center: products used to manage and produce live streams and fan-engagement experiences. Kiswe builds technology around cloud production, streaming, distribution, and interactive digital experiences. The problems are niche and interesting: complex interfaces, real-time constraints, and the added pressure that comes with anything live.

In the team, I am both a front-end specialist and a bridge between product and engineering. I pushed for building our own shared component library to keep the look and feel consistent across products, and took the lead on that library: defining patterns, shaping component APIs, and making sure we solve problems once in a reusable way.

Across every company I have worked at, I have ended up as the go-to person for CSS: the one people bring strange layout issues and styling bugs to when they do not make sense at first glance.

## The way I work

### 01 / Complex tools, simple experiences

I enjoy complex, interactive tools behind the scenes, but I want the experience to feel calm and understandable for the person using it.

### 02 / Clean, maintainable, readable code

I like solving hard UI problems, but I care just as much about leaving code behind that someone else can step into without pain.

### 03 / Bridging design and development

I am comfortable talking in both visual and technical terms. I enjoy turning design ideas into well-structured components and moving a product toward a coherent system instead of a collection of one-off solutions.

### 04 / Product-minded and UX-focused

I do not only ask “how do we build this?” but also “why are we building this?” and “how will it feel in real use?”. I like thinking ahead and doing the extra work that makes a product easier to extend and nicer to use later.

What keeps me motivated is that the products I work on are live and alive. They evolve. Knowing that decisions I make today will either pay off or come back to haunt me makes me care about structure, quality, and UX.

> The best work is rarely a single clever solution. It is a series of decisions that keep making sense as the product grows.

## Side projects and creative experiments

Outside my day job, I like mixing code, visuals, and sound.

A few examples:
- [Video Projection Mapping](https://github.com/arnedecant/video-projection-mapping) ([Live demo](https://arnedecant.github.io/video-projection-mapping/))
  Experiments with projecting visuals onto physical spaces and shapes using web technologies. It is a playground for mixing digital visuals with the physical world.
- [WebGL Webcam Audio Visualizer](https://github.com/arnedecant/audio-visualiser) ([Live demo](https://dev.arnedecant.be/webgl/webcam-audio-visualizer/))
  This app takes a camera feed and turns it into a particle system. Each particle responds to the colour of a specific video pixel and to the music being played. It is part WebGL and real-time graphics experiment, part excuse to combine audio, visuals, and interactivity.

I also like poking at smaller WebGL and audio-reactive experiments whenever something interesting pops into my head.
